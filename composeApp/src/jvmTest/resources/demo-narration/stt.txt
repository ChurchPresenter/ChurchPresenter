intro = Welcome to ChurchPresenter. In this tour, we'll put live captions on screen with the S T T tab, so everyone can follow the sermon, including people who are hard of hearing or who speak another language.
what = S T T stands for speech to text. A speech-to-text server listens to the preacher's microphone and turns what's said into written words as they're spoken. ChurchPresenter connects to that server and shows the words as captions.
server = It works with a Whisper-based speech-to-text server on another computer on your church network. We recommend running that server on a second computer, not the one running ChurchPresenter. Transcription puts a heavy load on a machine, and a separate computer keeps ChurchPresenter from freezing in the middle of a service.
show-tab = The S T T tab is hidden at first. To show it, click the Tab visibility button in the tab bar, and tick S T T in the list.
open-tab = To start, open the S T T tab in the main window.
url = Enter the address of your speech-to-text server in the S T T Server URL field.
connect = Then click Connect. The status line under it tells you whether it's connecting, connected, or can't reach the server. If the connection drops during a service, it reconnects by itself.
preview = Once it's connected, the live preview fills in as the preacher speaks. Finished sentences appear in full, and the words still being spoken show up as they come.
translation = If your server also translates, the translation runs right underneath. Here, the sermon is spoken in English and translated into Spanish as it goes. Expect the translation to trail by about five to ten seconds, because the server waits to hear a full sentence before translating it. How quickly it appears depends on how fast the speaker talks.
go-live = When you're ready, click Go Live, and the captions appear on your screens, word by word.
watch = Here's how it looks on the projector, with the English words and the Spanish translation shown together.
detect = The settings button next to Connect turns on scripture detection. With it on, the Bible tab listens to the captions and follows along, staging each verse as it's read out, and sending only clear matches straight to the screen.
look = How the captions look is set for each screen, in Settings, under Profiles, Live captions.
presentation = Choose how they're presented: a rolling transcript, pop-on blocks that fill and then clear, a one-line ticker along the bottom, or a few words at a time in the same spot.
shape = Show them in a card, or in a full-width band across the screen.
reading = There's plenty to make them easier to read. Captions can fade out after a silence, words can be held to a reading speed limit, new lines can slide up smoothly, and older lines can dim.
languages = With a translation, show both languages stacked, side by side, or with each line followed by its translation.
tips = Two tips before you start. Use a microphone close to the speaker, like a headset or a lapel mic, for the cleanest text. And test with a few real sentences before the service begins.
outro = And that's the S T T tab: live captions and translation, on every screen, for everyone in the room.
